Research

MedicalQA — Caregiver QA Generation & Evaluation

AIM Lab, Santa Clara University · ongoing

LLMsRAGReact/ViteSupabase

Family caregivers of people with dementia rarely have time to sit through hours of training videos. MedicalQA extends the AIM Lab's MentorQA framework into an LLM system that generates caregiver question–answer pairs directly from dementia-care training videos, so the guidance in them becomes searchable and conversational. Generation quality is compared across several pipelines: a single-agent baseline, multi-agent chunking, topic segmentation, and retrieval-augmented generation.

My contributions: I brought in the Teepa Snow dementia-care video dataset, added reproducibility controls (seeding, temperature, and decoding configuration) so pipeline comparisons are apples-to-apples, and built the human-annotation web app (React/Vite + Supabase, deployed on Netlify) that annotators use to rate generated QA pairs. I then analyzed the human judgments collected over the supported training videos to validate the evaluation rubric itself.

McMiner — Misconception Mining for LLM Code Tutoring

AIM Lab, Santa Clara University · ongoing · co-developed

LLMspass@kEvoCodeBenchPython

When an LLM tutors a weaker model (or a student) on a coding task, generic hints waste rounds. McMiner is a "misconception-in-the-loop" tutoring method built on top of TRAVER: the tutor first poses a diagnostic completion, mines the student's specific misconception from the attempt, and injects that misconception into the next tutoring round — so every hint targets what the student actually got wrong.

We evaluate with pass@1/5/10 on the EvoCodeBench dataset, broken down by task difficulty and tutoring round, baseline versus McMiner. Because multi-round evaluation over a full benchmark takes many GPU-hours, I built a fault-tolerant evaluation harness on GPU pods that auto-pauses and resumes across multi-hour model-server outages instead of losing runs. I also built a demo web app that shows baseline and McMiner tutoring dialogues side by side.

Medical Anomaly Detection with Vision-Language Models

CLIPLoRAPyTorch

Labeled medical imaging data is scarce, so this project asks how far a general vision-language model can go on chest X-rays with only a few labeled examples. Starting from CLIP, I applied LoRA fine-tuning on the RSNA dataset (26k+ chest X-ray images) for few-shot anomaly detection.

I implemented and compared multiple anomaly-scoring methods on top of the adapted model, then improved performance further through model fusion and regularization.

TTS & Voice Imitation

B.S. graduation thesis (team of six), Alexandria University · 2019

Tacotron 2Speech SynthesisDeep Learning

A neural text-to-speech system that generates speech in the voice of different speakers — including speakers never seen during training. The system consists of three separately trained models built around a Tacotron 2 synthesizer: it takes text plus a reference voice and produces speech in that voice. We also built a simple app to interact with the model.

Beyond the English models, we added an Egyptian-dialect Arabic dataset and fine-tuned the system to generate realistic Arabic speech — a language where dialectal TTS training resources are scarce. The slides below link to generated voice samples you can listen to.
